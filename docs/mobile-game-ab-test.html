Cookie Cats — Moving the First Gate

A/B test in a mobile puzzle game · Group A: gate at level 30 · Group B: gate at level 40 · 90,189 new players

Keep the first gate at level 30. Moving it to level 40 did not improve retention: 7-day retention is −0.82 pp for Group B (95% CI −1.33 to −0.31 pp). But the test has a sample-ratio mismatch (p = 0.009) and, once it is accounted for, the real effect is smaller and less certain than the headline suggests.

Retention

Share of players who came back 1 and 7 days after install

Sample Ratio

Players per group: under 30 rounds (never reached level 30) vs 30+ rounds (could reach it)

Groups should be 50/50, but Group B received 789 more players (p = 0.009). The imbalance comes entirely from players who never reached level 30 (+832 in Group B, p = 0.0005), while players with 30+ rounds are balanced (43 players apart, p = 0.81). For players who never reach level 30 the game is identical in both groups, so the gate cannot explain this — it points to an assignment or logging issue that adds low-engagement players to Group B and mechanically lowers its average retention.

Who Saw the Gate

Share of players by game rounds played in the first 14 days

63.1% of players played fewer than 30 rounds in their first 14 days, so they never reached level 30 and never saw either gate. Including them dilutes the effect (intent-to-treat). For them the two groups should be identical — a built-in placebo check. Every level takes at least one round, and the game is the same in both groups until level 30, so this split is not biased by the treatment itself.

Effect by Segment

Group B − Group A, 7-day retention, percentage points with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals

Placebo: among players who never reached the gate, Group B still shows 7-day retention −0.33 pp (95% CI −0.65 to −0.01 pp). It should be zero — another sign the groups are not fully comparable.
Among players who could reach the gate, the test could reliably detect (80% power) only D7 differences of 1.5 pp or more; the observed difference is 0.87 pp. To confirm an effect of that size you would need about 275k players in total — 3.0× this test.

Conclusions

  • ✗Moving the gate to level 40 did not improve retention. Day-1 retention: 44.8% vs 44.2% (not significant); day-7: 19.0% vs 18.2%.
  • ⚠Sample-ratio mismatch (p = 0.009): Group B got 832 extra players who never reached level 30. The headline D7 gap of −0.82 pp shrinks to −0.53 pp after adjusting for group composition.
  • ⚠Placebo check fails at the margin: players who never saw a gate differ by −0.33 pp on D7. Part of the remaining gap is likely not caused by the gate.
  • –Among players who could reach the gate (36.9% of players), the D7 difference is −0.87 pp (95% CI −1.93 to +0.18 pp), probability that Group B is better 5%. Day-1 retention is unchanged (+0.09 pp).
  • –Group B players with 30+ rounds played slightly more rounds (median 75 vs 72) — expected, since they get 10 more levels before the gate.
Recommendations
  • Keep the gate at level 30 — there is no evidence that level 40 helps, and the data leans towards it hurting 7-day retention.
  • Audit the assignment before trusting the size of the effect: check how players were bucketed, bot/duplicate installs and event logging for low-activity players in Group B.
  • If the decision is worth it, re-run with fixed assignment and plan the sample for the effect size that matters (about 275k players to detect a 0.9 pp D7 change among exposed players).

Method. Retention differences: bootstrap (10,000 resamples) 95% CIs, two-proportion z-test p-values, Bayesian probability that Group B is better (Beta(1,1) prior). Game rounds: winsorized at the pooled 99th percentile (one player logged ~50k rounds) and Mann-Whitney test. Sample ratio: χ² test against a 50/50 split. Composition-adjusted estimate: within-segment differences weighted by the pooled segment shares. Power: two-proportion normal approximation, α = 0.05, 80% power.

Data. "Mobile Games: A/B Testing" (Cookie Cats), public educational dataset originally from a DataCamp project, published on Kaggle. Used for demonstration only.
