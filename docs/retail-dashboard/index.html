Key findings (all markets)

  • Revenue grew +2.5% year over year while orders fell 4.0%: growth came from bigger baskets (average order value +6.8%), not from more orders.
  • Returning customers bring 78% of revenue; first-time customers only 8%. Keeping existing customers matters far more to revenue here than acquiring new ones.
  • About 20% of new customers buy again in each of the following four months (cohort median); by months 9–12 it drops to 11%–15%.
  • Guest checkouts (no customer ID) grew from 11% to 15% of revenue — a growing share of sales that cannot be retargeted or tracked for retention.
  • Seasonality is strong: revenue peaks in Nov ahead of the holidays. The UK is 85% of revenue; Netherlands, Ireland, Germany lead international sales.

Data & method. 1.07M invoice lines, Dec 2009 – Dec 2011. Removed: exact duplicate rows (the source sheets overlap), cancellations and bad-debt adjustments, non-product lines (postage, fees, manual adjustments, gift cards), zero/negative quantities and prices, and the partial month Dec 2011. Revenue = quantity × unit price (GBP). Customer metrics use lines with a customer ID. Cohorts start Jan 2010 — customers first seen in Dec 2009 may have bought before the data begins.
